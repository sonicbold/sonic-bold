The Denver plumbing market

Denver sits about a mile above sea level, winters bring hard freezes, and many homes have basements. All three shape which plumbing jobs come in and when.

Seasonality. Cold snaps bring frozen and burst pipe calls, often at night and on weekends, when an office is least likely to pick up. Fall brings sprinkler winterization and the leaks that show up when a system was not drained in time. Spring thaw and heavy storms push water into basements, so sump pump, floor drain and sewer backup calls rise.

Altitude. At Denver's elevation, gas appliances like water heaters have to be set up for high altitude, which fuel gas codes call derating. That gives a licensed plumber a real edge over a handyman on water heater installs.

Water. Denver Water describes its water as soft to moderately hard, a little harder in winter and softer from late spring through fall. Scale still builds up over time, but softener installs are less of a default upsell here than in a hard-water market like Phoenix.

Housing stock. Older neighborhoods close to downtown have many early twentieth century homes with aging supply lines and older sewer laterals, where tree roots and cracked pipe lead to sewer scopes, hydro jetting and line replacement. Denver Water says homes built before 1951 are more likely to have lead service lines, and it runs a Lead Reduction Program that replaces customer-owned lead lines with copper. Newer suburbs bring a different mix: water heater replacements, finished-basement plumbing and remodels.

Licensing. Colorado licenses plumbers at the state level through the State Plumbing Board, part of the Department of Regulatory Agencies. Individual plumbers hold residential, journeyworker or master licenses, and a plumbing contractor must register with the board and either be or employ a full-time master plumber. Since mid-2025, Colorado also requires contractors to show their contractor registration number and their responsible master plumber's license number on their vehicles, billing materials, bid sheets and website. Inside the City and County of Denver, plumbing contractors also need a Denver contractor license from Community Planning and Development, and Denver does not accept licenses from other counties or states.

Local Services Ads for Denver plumbers

Local Services Ads are the Google Guaranteed listings at the very top of the results, and you pay per lead rather than per click. Our Local Services Ads for plumbers page explains the basics. This is what matters in Denver.

Verification. Google screens plumbers more heavily than most categories. Expect business and owner checks, background checks on the people who go into customers' homes, proof of insurance, and a license check. In Colorado, have your State Plumbing Board contractor registration number and your responsible master plumber's license number ready, and make sure the business name on your state record, your Denver license, your Google Business Profile and your LSA application all match. Name mismatches are the usual reason applications stall, so we compare them before anything goes in. Background checks run on Google's schedule, and we tell you what is pending rather than guess a date.

Bad leads and credits. Google no longer takes manual lead disputes. Since mid-2024 it reviews leads automatically and credits ones it judges invalid or low quality, like spam and duplicates. Leads for job types or areas you don't serve aren't credited, so your settings are your real protection. Winter still brings a lot of calls you cannot take: a furnace that will not light, a frozen pipe in a town an hour away, or a request for a service you do not offer. We review leads weekly, send feedback through Google's lead survey, and tighten ZIP codes and job types when the same miss repeats.

How reviews affect ranking. Google says LSA ranking depends on factors including how close you are to the customer, your reviews, your business hours and how quickly you respond to leads. Nobody can promise a top spot, and we will not. What we can do is set up a simple review request after every finished job, make sure your listed hours match when someone really answers, and keep an eye on response time. During a cold snap, the plumber who picks up fast and has fresh reviews gets chosen. Our post on how missed calls turn into lost jobs covers the phone side.

Google search ads for Denver plumbers

Search ads let you choose which searches you show up for. In Denver they work best when emergency work and planned work are kept apart.

Emergency searches. Burst pipe, frozen pipe, sewer backup, flooded basement, no hot water, water heater leaking. These people want someone at the door today. We run them as call-focused ads, schedule them around the hours your phones are covered, and turn up coverage during freezes instead of running the same settings all year.

Planned jobs. Water heater replacement, tankless installs, sewer line replacement, sewer camera inspections, repiping, lead service line questions, and basement or bathroom remodel plumbing. These buyers compare and ask for quotes, so they get their own campaigns and are measured differently.

Cutting wasted spend. Each week we review the actual searches that triggered your ads and block the ones that will never become jobs, such as do-it-yourself questions, job seekers, parts shoppers, and heating-only searches if you do not do heating. Denver plumber questions

What license do I need to run Local Services Ads as a plumber in Colorado?

Google checks plumbing licenses at the state level. In Colorado that means your State Plumbing Board records: the plumbing contractor registration and the master plumber license tied to it. The business name on those records should match your Google application.

Do I need a separate license to work inside Denver?

Yes. The City and County of Denver requires plumbing contractors to hold a Denver contractor license from Community Planning and Development, on top of the state registration, and it does not accept licenses from other counties or states. Surrounding cities may have their own rules, so check each one you plan to advertise in.

Do my license numbers need to be on my ads landing page?

Colorado requires plumbing contractors to show their contractor registration number and their responsible master plumber's license number on their website and advertising. We check that your landing pages include both before launch.

Can you push harder during cold snaps?

Yes. Frozen and burst pipe searches jump when temperatures drop, so we raise emergency coverage during freezes and pull it back when your schedule is full or your phones are not covered.

Can you cover Aurora, Lakewood, Littleton and the rest of the metro?

Yes. We set the service area around where you hold licenses and where your trucks can get to, whether that is central Denver only or the whole metro from Brighton to Castle Rock.

How does the free 30 days work?

Your 30 days start when your campaigns go live. We manage Google Ads and LSA with no management fee during that time, and you pay Google for ad spend. After that, management is month to month.
