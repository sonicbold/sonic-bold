How Much Should a Plumbing Company Spend on Google Ads?

By Sam • Updated September 14, 2026 • 8-minute read

Quick answer: A 1–5 van plumbing company commonly starts around $1.5k–$3.5k per month in Google Ads spend, then adjusts from actual qualified calls, service-area demand, and booked-job economics. There is no universal budget; the right number is the amount you can spend while tracking lead quality and answering the calls you generate.

The goal of a plumbing Google Ads budget is not to buy the most clicks. It is to create enough qualified demand for your team to answer, schedule, and profitably serve. A smaller campaign with tight targeting can outperform a larger campaign that pays for DIY searches, job seekers, or customers outside your service area.

Start with service area and competition

A dense metro with many advertisers will usually require more budget than a smaller market. Before setting a number, list the cities and ZIP codes you can serve profitably, then separate emergency services from lower-urgency work. Emergency plumbing, sewer repair, and water-heater replacement often deserve their own campaigns because the customer intent and value are different.

  • Define the exact service radius and exclude areas you cannot reach quickly.
  • Prioritize the services that can support your target cost per booked job.
  • Reserve budget for the hours when your phone is answered.

Use a simple budget formula

A useful planning formula is:

Monthly ad budget = target booked jobs × acceptable ad cost per booked job

For example, if you want 10 additional booked jobs and can spend $250 in advertising for each booked job, the planning budget is $2,500. Your actual numbers will vary, so treat the first 60–90 days as a measurement period rather than a promise of a fixed return.

Separate ad spend from management fees

Your Google Ads budget is paid to Google. Agency management, landing-page work, call tracking, and other services are separate operating costs. Keep those numbers distinct so you can see the true cost of acquiring and booking a customer.

Do not scale before tracking qualified calls

A lead is not automatically a good lead. Track whether calls are inside your service area, whether the customer needs a service you offer, whether the call was answered, and whether it became a booked job. Once those signals are connected to campaigns and search terms—typically through plumbing call tracking—budget can move toward what produces revenue instead of what only produces form fills.

Frequently asked questions

Is $1.5k a month enough for a plumbing Google Ads campaign?

It can be a reasonable starting point in some markets, especially when the campaign is limited to high-intent services and a focused service area. The budget may need to increase in more competitive cities.

Should I spend more on Google Ads or Local Services Ads?

That depends on lead quality, availability, and how much control you need. Test both when appropriate, but track them separately so you know which channel creates qualified and booked work. See our LSA vs. Search Ads guide and the Google Ads for plumbers service page.

How long should I test a budget?

Give the campaign enough time to collect search-term, call-quality, and booking data. Avoid making major decisions from a few days of clicks or from impressions alone.
