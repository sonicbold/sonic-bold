Revenue & Call Tracking

How to Track Google Ads All the Way to Booked Plumbing Jobs

By Sam • Updated September 14, 2026 • 7-minute read

Quick answer: Plumbing companies should track the full path from keyword to click, call, qualified lead, booked job, and revenue. Call tracking, form tracking, CRM or dispatch-board status, and consistent source data make that connection possible.

Clicks and impressions are useful diagnostics, but they are not the final business outcome. A plumbing campaign can look successful in an ad platform while producing calls outside your service area, price shoppers, or leads nobody answers. Revenue tracking closes that gap. Sonic Bold’s plumbing call tracking work is built around that path for Google Ads for plumbers.

1. Track the source of every inquiry

Start by recording the campaign, ad group, search term, landing page, and device connected to each form submission and phone call. Dynamic numbers, call extensions, tagged links, and a consistent naming convention make the data easier to reconcile later.

  • Use a unique tracking number for paid search when appropriate.
  • Use UTM parameters on forms and booking links.
  • Keep Google Ads, analytics, call tracking, and dispatch labels consistent.

2. Define a qualified plumbing lead

A qualified lead should have a clear definition. For example: the caller is in the service area, needs a service you provide, and is looking to schedule or receive immediate help. A missed call, a vendor solicitation, and a DIY question should not be counted the same way as a booked emergency repair.

3. Connect leads to the dispatch board

The most valuable step is matching the marketing source to the job record. Whether the team uses a CRM, dispatch software, or a simple shared system, record the lead status: new, contacted, qualified, booked, completed, cancelled, or lost. This creates a feedback loop for budget decisions.

Example: If one keyword produces 20 calls but only one booked job, while another produces 8 calls and four booked jobs, the second keyword deserves closer attention even though it has less call volume.

4. Measure the right numbers

A useful monthly report should separate:

  • Ad spend and cost per click
  • Calls and forms
  • Qualified leads
  • Booked jobs and completed jobs
  • Cost per qualified lead and cost per booked job
  • Revenue or estimated gross profit where available

This prevents a low cost per lead from hiding poor lead quality. It also gives the owner and campaign manager a shared language for deciding what to pause, improve, or scale.

5. Improve response time after the call

Tracking cannot repair a missed call by itself. Make sure the phone rings where someone can answer, after-hours coverage is defined, and the caller receives a clear next step. If a call is not answered, record it as a missed opportunity rather than treating it as a successful conversion.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need a CRM to track booked jobs?

No. A CRM makes the workflow easier, but a consistent spreadsheet or dispatch process can work while volume is small. The important part is recording source and status consistently.

What is more important: calls or booked jobs?

Booked jobs are closer to the business outcome. Calls are still useful, but they should be segmented by quality, answer status, and whether they became work.

How often should tracking be reviewed?

Review call quality and tracking errors weekly, then review booked-job economics monthly. Faster checks catch broken forms, missed calls, or irrelevant search terms before they waste budget.
