Clicks lie. Booked jobs do not.

A plumbing Google Ads account can look “healthy” on CTR while quietly buying:

  • DIY researchers
  • Job seekers
  • Out-of-area tire kickers
  • Callers who never book
  • Callers who book then cancel

If your report stops at clicks or even raw calls, you are managing the wrong KPI. Sonic Bold orients tracking toward qualified calls and booked jobs—the language owners actually run a shop on.

Dynamic Number Insertion (DNI)

DNI swaps phone numbers by traffic source so a caller can be tied back to the campaign/keyword/path that created the call.

That is how you learn:

  • Which keywords deserve budget
  • Which ads waste money
  • Which landing pages convert
  • Whether brand search is helping or just harvesting existing demand

Without DNI (or careful number discipline), everything collapses into “the phone rang.”

Call recording and CSR coaching

Recordings are not about surveillance theater. They are how you:

  • Spot junk leads early
  • Coach greeting and qualification
  • Catch missed booking opportunities
  • Hear whether “bad leads” are actually bad CSRs

Plumbing margins die in the first 30 seconds of a call as often as they die in the auction.

Dispatch software matching

We connect tracking data with tools plumbing companies already use—ServiceTitan, Housecall Pro, Jobber, and similar—where access and workflow allow, to report cost-per-booked-job and clearer return on ad spend.

Perfect attribution is rare. Directionally honest attribution beats decorative dashboards. The goal is decisions: keep, cut, or creative-test.

Cost per booked job as the north star

Optimize toward:

  1. Cost per qualified call
  2. Cost per booked job
  3. Revenue / gross margin contribution when you can close the loop

Then budget conversations get sane. See Google Ads Budget for Plumbers.

Number strategy must be intentional. Randomly posting five different numbers across GBP, LSA, ads, and the truck wraps will break your story.

Pair tracking with conversion assets: Landing Pages and answer coverage via AI Receptionist.

Implementation process

  1. Audit current numbers, tags, call flows, and CRM/dispatch fields
  2. Design source map (Ads / LSA / GBP / LPs)
  3. Implement DNI + recording where appropriate
  4. Define qualified call criteria with you
  5. Connect booked-job outcomes as tightly as the stack allows
  6. Review weekly inside founder-led optimization—not quarterly archaeology

Engagements for media remain month-to-month, flat fee, accounts you own. Tracking setup may include implementation scope—quoted honestly, no fake “unlimited attribution” promises.

Who this is for

Plumbing owners running paid demand who are done arguing about leads without job data.

Not for: companies unwilling to record calls (where lawful) or unwilling to define what “qualified” means.

Building a plumbing-friendly reporting rhythm

Weekly review (short):

  • Top spend campaigns/keywords
  • Qualified call count and rate
  • Obvious junk themes for negatives
  • Missed-call / after-hours notes
  • LP conversion outliers

Monthly review (deeper):

  • Cost per qualified call trends
  • Cost per booked job by channel (Ads vs LSA vs other)
  • Service-type mix (are we buying the work we want?)
  • Geo profitability notes
  • CSR coaching themes from recordings

If reporting takes longer to read than it takes to fix something, it is too long.

Qualified call definitions you can actually use

Write them down. Example starter set:

  • In area
  • Residential (or your ICP)
  • Service you offer
  • Not a vendor/spam
  • Conversation long enough to evaluate (align with your tracker's rules)
  • Not a job seeker

Then train CSRs to tag outcomes the same way every time. Inconsistent tags destroy “cost per booked job” math.

Multi-number strategy pitfalls

Common failure modes:

  • GBP primary number swapped carelessly for tracking
  • LSA number not reconciled with call software
  • Website showing a different number than ads
  • Trucks and invoices teaching customers an untracked number only

Create a number map document and treat it like production plumbing—label everything.

Privacy, notice, and trust

Call recording laws vary. Use clear notices where required. Homeowners already anxious about a flood do not need a creepy surprise. Transparency is part of brand.

Your privacy policy and SMS/A2P terms should stay aligned with whatever messaging/call tools you enable.

How tracking changes optimization behavior

With real call data, Sonic Bold management focuses on:

  • Negatives from actual junk transcripts
  • Budgets toward keywords that book
  • LP tests tied to call rate, not bounce myths
  • Recommendations to pause geos that only produce unprofitable rides

Without tracking, agencies argue opinions. With tracking, you argue evidence.

Connecting tracking to growth decisions

Tracking is how you answer:

  • Should we raise budget?
  • Should we hire another CSR or tech?
  • Is LSA or Search carrying booked jobs?
  • Is after-hours coverage paying for itself?

Those are owner decisions. Our job is to make the numbers trustworthy enough to decide. Pair with Google Ads, LSA, and AI receptionist when the data says answer rate is the bottleneck.

Hand-off package for your office manager

Give the office a one-page SOP:

  • How to see today’s calls by source
  • How to mark qualified / booked / junk
  • How to flag dispute candidates for LSA
  • How to escalate tracking breakage (wrong number on site)
  • Who owns weekly review with Sonic Bold

Tools fail when only the agency understands them. Founder-led management includes teaching your people the cockpit—not hiding it.

First 14 days after install — what “done” means

Tracking is not done when the tag fires once. Done means: test calls from ads LP, organic/GBP path, and a known direct dial; recordings available; sources labeled correctly; CSR tags working; a sample booked job visible in the monthly story. Until that checklist clears, do not make big budget decisions off the new numbers.

If you only remember one line: optimize to booked jobs, or you will keep arguing about clicks that never paid a tech.

QUESTIONS & ANSWERS

Frequently asked questions

Is call tracking legal?

Compliance depends on jurisdiction and notice requirements. We implement with proper disclosure practices—confirm your local rules.

Will DNI hurt my GBP SEO?

Number strategy should be planned carefully. We do not recklessly swap your primary GBP NAP without a plan.

Do I need this if I only run LSA?

Yes—answer rate, disputes, and booked outcomes still need visibility.

Can you guarantee ROAS?

No. We build measurement so you can manage toward profitable jobs.
