DATA & ATTRIBUTION

Revenue & Call Tracking for Plumbing Companies.

Connect marketing spend directly to your dispatch board. Stop guessing and start optimizing based on real booked revenue.

Get a Free Audit View Client Results

Clicks are not the product. A plumbing company can win the auction, pay for the visit, and still have no idea which keyword put a water-heater job on the board. Sonic Bold installs tracking that follows the path keyword → call → qualified lead → booked job so you can spend against revenue, not vanity CTR.

This is the measurement layer behind Google Ads and LSA. Without it, weekly optimization is guesswork—and agencies can hide behind dashboards that look green while the dispatch board stays thin. See How to Track Google Ads to Booked Plumbing Jobs.

Dynamic number insertion

DNI swaps the phone number a visitor sees based on traffic source. A Google Ads click can show a different number than a Maps visitor. When the phone rings, you know which campaign and, in many setups, which keyword earned the conversation. That is how you stop scaling a cheap term that never books and start defending the expensive term that does.

Recordings and CSR coaching

A “lead” that is a parts vendor, a DIY question, or a caller outside the radius should not be celebrated in a monthly PDF. Recordings let you score quality, coach how the office books (or loses) emergencies, and dispute junk on LSA with evidence. If after-hours coverage is weak, recordings make that obvious—see AI receptionist.

Dispatch software matching

The last mile is matching the marketing source to the job record in ServiceTitan, Housecall Pro, Jobber, or the system you already run. We do not invent a CRM you do not need. We do insist on a consistent status: new, contacted, qualified, booked, completed, cancelled, lost. That is what turns call volume into cost per booked job.

Example of the kind of decision this unlocks: one keyword produces a pile of calls and almost no bookings; another produces fewer calls and several booked jobs. Volume is not the winner. The second keyword is.

What a useful report contains

  • Ad spend and cost per click (diagnostics, not the scoreboard)
  • Calls and forms, split by answered vs missed
  • Qualified leads vs junk / out-of-area / wrong service
  • Booked and completed jobs
  • Cost per qualified lead and cost per booked job
  • Revenue or estimated gross profit only where your books already support it—we will not fabricate ROI

Budget decisions get cleaner once this exists. How much to spend is a capacity and cost-per-job question, not a CPC target copied from a Facebook group.

Commercial terms

Tracking setup is scoped to your stack. Ongoing management stays month-to-month and flat-fee. You own the numbers, the call recordings policy, and the Google accounts. We will not optimize for cheaper clicks that do not book.

What “qualified” means in a plumbing account

Agree on the definition before you judge a campaign. A useful default: the caller is in the service area, needs a service you offer, and wants to schedule or get emergency help. A missed ring is not a conversion. A vendor, a DIY how-to, and a shopper collecting five quotes are not the same as a booked water-heater leak.

Once that definition is shared, weekly search-term reviews and monthly booked-job reviews have a language both the owner and the specialist can use. That is how budget moves toward work worth doing—see the budget framework.

Privacy, recordings, and office habits

Call recording is a coaching tool, not a gotcha. Tell the office it exists. Follow the consent and notice rules that apply to your states. Keep recordings long enough to dispute junk and train CSRs, not forever “just in case.” Sonic Bold will not publish your calls or invent conversion rates from them.

If after-hours coverage is the leak, tracking will show missed night calls clearly. Fix the answer path with a live service or an AI receptionist rather than buying more daytime clicks you already cannot staff.

How this sits next to landing pages and LSA

DNI on a campaign landing page tells you which ad and keyword produced the ring. LSA has its own lead inbox and dispute flow; we still want booked-job status so you are not comparing “LSA leads” to “Search booked jobs” as if they were the same unit. The scoreboard is the dispatch board.

Who this is for

Owners who are done arguing about CTR in a PDF. If you will not listen to recordings or mark jobs as booked vs lost, tracking will not save the account—it will only make the leak visible. That visibility is the point. We work with 1–10 van plumbing shops that want founder-led media and honest numbers, not a vanity dashboard from a multi-trade agency.

If the leak is the page after the click, read the PPC landing page checklist. If the leak is the channel mix, start with LSA vs Search.

We will not invent ROAS, cost-per-job averages for your city, or Core Web Vitals scores. The first deliverable is a path you can audit: this keyword, this call, this job status. Once that exists, optimization has something real to push against. Request that review from contact.

QUESTIONS & ANSWERS

Frequently asked questions

What is dynamic number insertion?

DNI swaps the visible phone number based on traffic source so a Google Ads call can be tied back to the campaign and keyword that caused it.

Do I need ServiceTitan, Housecall Pro, or Jobber?

Those stacks make booked-job matching easier, but the first step is clean call source data and a consistent job status. We work with the system you already use.

Is a cheap cost-per-lead a good sign?

Not by itself. Cheap calls that never book are still waste. We optimize toward qualified calls and booked jobs.

Will you listen to call recordings?

Yes, as part of quality review and CSR coaching—so junk leads and missed booking moments are visible.

How does this change reporting?

Monthly reviews talk in plumbing language: calls, qualified leads, booked jobs, and cost per booked job—not vanity CTR alone.
