AFTER-HOURS CALL CAPTURE

Never Miss Another After-Hours Emergency Call.

Capture high-ticket jobs while you sleep. Our AI receptionist answers in 2 rings, qualifies the emergency, and notifies your team instantly.

Get a Free Audit View Client Results

Plumbing emergencies do not wait for office hours. A homeowner with a burst pipe will call the next listing if your line rings out. If you are already paying for Google Ads or Local Services Ads, every after-hours voicemail is a lead you bought for a competitor.

Sonic Bold’s 24/7 AI receptionist is built for plumbing dispatch—not a generic chatbot bolted onto a website. It answers the phone, qualifies the emergency, captures the address and callback, and alerts your on-call tech so the job does not die in a mailbox.

Why missed calls wreck paid media

Search and LSA both assume someone will pick up. Google’s LSA ranking habits in particular punish slow answer rates. Even when ranking holds, the homeowner does not wait. The sales cycle for a flooded kitchen is measured in rings, not business days. Read Why Plumbing Companies Lose Jobs to Slower Competitors.

A live CSR during the day is still the ideal. AI coverage is for nights, weekends, holidays, and the stretch when every tech is under a house. It does not replace a good dispatcher. It stops the leak when that dispatcher cannot get to the phone.

What the receptionist actually does

  • Answers quickly with a professional, plumbing-specific script
  • Collects address, callback number, and a short description of the emergency
  • Qualifies whether the job is in-area and the type of work you want
  • Pushes a summary to your on-call tech by SMS or email, with recording and transcript when enabled
  • Keeps a record you can review next to call tracking so after-hours demand is visible

You set the rules: service area, after-hours fees you want mentioned, jobs you do not take, and when a human must take over.

How this fits Search and LSA

If LSA lead quality looks “bad,” listen to the recordings before you blame the channel. A share of those leads were real emergencies that hit voicemail. Search Ads have the same problem with a different invoice: you paid for the click, then nobody answered.

We treat answer coverage as part of the acquisition system—same as negatives and landing pages—not as a shiny add-on. Exclusive demand from Google is wasted if the first human voice the homeowner hears is a competitor.

Who this is for

Owner-operators and small teams (roughly 1–10 vans) who already buy or plan to buy exclusive Google demand, and who know nights and weekends are when the expensive jobs show up. It is optional. If your office already answers around the clock, we will not sell you a tool you do not need.

Commercial terms stay the same as the rest of Sonic Bold: founder-led, month-to-month, no percentage of ad spend. Reception coverage is scoped separately from media management so you are not paying for theater.

What we script—and what we will not claim

The script should sound like a plumbing company, not a call center reading a menu. We collect the facts a tech needs: where the water is, whether it is still running, whether someone is home, and whether the address is in the radius you actually cover. We will not promise a 45-minute arrival if your board cannot support it. We will not book drain-cleaning specials you do not want at 2 a.m. unless you say so.

“Answers in two rings” is the operating target for the product, not a published Core Web Vitals score. If a setup cannot meet that in your environment, we say so on the audit. Recordings exist so you can hear whether the bot sounded helpful or robotic and tighten the prompts.

Handoff to a human

Overflow during the day should escalate to a live CSR when one is available. After hours, the alert has to reach a person who can accept or decline the job. If nobody ever looks at the SMS, you have voicemail with extra steps. We treat that as an operations problem, not a software trophy.

Pair the receptionist with call tracking so after-hours inquiries still have a source. Pair it with LSA if Google is already sending leads you miss at night. Pair it with Search Ads if you are paying for emergency keywords that ring an empty shop.

When not to buy this

If your spouse already answers every night and weekends are fully staffed, spend the money on cleaner keywords or a better landing page instead. If you cannot take more work, answering more phones only creates angry homeowners. Capacity first, then capture.

How we decide it is working

Success is not “the bot talked to someone.” Success is after-hours inquiries that become booked jobs you wanted, without filling Saturday with out-of-area or DIY calls. We review recordings the same way we review Search terms: keep what books, tighten what wastes a tech’s night. If the tool cannot do that in your market, we will not keep it on the invoice out of habit.

Related reading: missed calls and lost jobs, plus how exclusive Google demand differs from shared lead boards. If you are still deciding whether Search or LSA should fund those night calls, start with which channel first.

QUESTIONS & ANSWERS

Frequently asked questions

Does this replace a live dispatcher?

No. It covers the gaps—after hours, overflow, and the minutes when every tech is on a job—so paid leads are not sent to voicemail.

Will it book junk jobs?

The script is built to qualify emergencies, service area, and the work you actually want. You set the rules.

How does this protect Google Ads and LSA spend?

A missed after-hours call is a paid lead you already bought. Faster answer coverage keeps that spend from funding the plumber who picks up.

Do I still need call tracking?

Yes. Recordings and source tracking show whether after-hours coverage is turning inquiries into booked jobs.

Is this required to work with Sonic Bold?

No. It is an option when missed calls are leaking jobs you already paid to generate.
